Guide & settings

How the analyzer works

What each step does, why it is done that way, and the settings that control it. Changes apply immediately and are saved in this browser.

1 · Overview

The analyzer turns raw tracer sweeps into a retest list. For every module it answers three questions:

  • How much power would it make at standard conditions? Sweeps are estimated at common reference conditions using assumed or characterized coefficients. Unvalidated translations are for screening, not certification.
  • Is it different from its siblings? Modules built from the same cells and process should be nearly identical. One that stands out is suspicious even if it meets spec.
  • Is it below what Ne3 cells should deliver? This catches a whole batch that is uniformly bad, which a population comparison alone cannot see.

Why not just compare raw Pmax? Two sweeps of the same module ten minutes apart can differ by 5–10 % because haze crossed the sun or the module warmed up. STC translation can help, but uncharacterized coefficients, illumination spectrum and cell temperature can still introduce systematic errors that the displayed U does not include.

Sharing criteria: Export settings writes a small JSON file. When teammates import it, everyone screens with the same coefficients and thresholds. Fields you have changed from the default are outlined in orange and show their default value.

2 · Taking a good measurement

No correction can rescue a poor sweep. Most bad screening results trace back to one of these:

  • Irradiance sensor coplanar with the module. Same tilt and direction, close by and unshaded. A few degrees of tilt mismatch changes the reading by a few percent, which goes straight into Pmax.
  • Stable, clear sky. At least W/m², ideally above 800. Read the irradiance just before and just after the sweep. If the two readings differ by more than about 1 %, sweep again.
  • Real cell temperature. Tape a thermocouple to the back, behind the centre of a cell, and cover it so wind does not cool it. Uncovered modules heat by several kelvin per minute, so sweep promptly and take every module the same way.
  • Full sweep. Cover short circuit (V ≈ 0) through open circuit (I ≈ 0) with at least points, concentrated around the knee if the tracer allows it.
  • Slow enough for back-contact cells. Maxeon cells have high capacitance. A sweep that is too fast distorts the curve, and forward and reverse sweeps then disagree. If they do, slow the sweep down.
  • Four-wire (Kelvin) connection. Use one if the tracer supports it. Otherwise lead and clip resistance shows up as low fill factor.
  • Correct Ns and Np. Count the cells. A wrong Ns appears as a wrong Voc per cell and a wrong W per cell.
  • Repeat sweeps. Use the same module ID to store multiple sweeps as one device; the app computes per-module means and a repeatability estimate without inflating the population sample size.
  • Same setup for the whole population. Use the same sensor, tracer and leads, on the same day if possible. Errors shared by every module then cancel when modules are compared with each other.

4 · STC estimation (IEC 60891:2021, Procedure 1 equations)

Engineering estimate, not IEC-certified testing. v3 implements the published Procedure 1 point-translation equations, but the default temperature, series-resistance and curve-shape coefficients are provisional. The uncertainty budget does not include coefficient/model error or spectral mismatch. Use measured raw Pmax and FF for primary diagnosis until coefficients are validated.

Inputs. Each sweep has measured G₁ and cell temperature T₁. Reference conditions are G₂ and T₂. The procedure uses absolute temperature coefficients α (A/K) and β (V/K), plus module series resistance Rs (Ω) and curve factor κ (Ω/K). The settings use per-cell/per-string values scaled by Ns and Np; that scaling is an approximation until measured for your assemblies.

I₂ = I₁ + Isc₁ · (G₂/G₁ − 1) + α · (T₂ − T₁) V₂ = V₁ − Rs · (I₂ − I₁) − κ · I₂ · (T₂ − T₁) + β · (T₂ − T₁) α = α_cell · Np · G₂/1000 β = β_cell · Ns Rs = Rs_cell · Ns/Np κ = κ_cell · Ns/Np

The G₂/1000 factor for α is an engineering approximation for moving a per-string STC coefficient to another target irradiance. For STC corrections it equals one. Rs and κ are module-specific correction parameters and should be identified from measured curves of the actual construction.

Missing corrected endpoints. A higher-irradiance translation shifts the measured Voc point to positive current. The analyzer uses a linear endpoint extrapolation from nearby points when needed, records it, and flags it when it exceeds configurable limits. Small corrections typically need limited endpoint extrapolation and are not automatically flagged. The standard discusses forward-bias measurements or polynomial fitting to improve corrected Voc determination.

Calibration. Measure several I–V curves at controlled, different irradiances and matched temperatures, then fit Rs by minimizing differences in the translated curves around the knee and power region. For κ, use measurements at several temperatures. Do not calibrate Rs by trying to align the exact Voc point; the Rs term vanishes when I₂ − I₁ = 0.

Validity. Prefer measurements near STC with stable uniform sunlight; Procedure 1 is best suited to limited translations. If a correction requires substantial extrapolation, retest near reference conditions. Do not interpret the calculated ±U as a validated accuracy statement.

Replace with measured coefficients when possible.

5 · Finding Pmax

A tracer samples the curve at discrete points, and the true maximum power point usually lies between two samples. Near its peak the power curve P(V) is very close to a parabola. The analyzer therefore fits a parabola through the highest sample and its two neighbours and takes the vertex.

  • The refinement is kept only if the vertex lies between the neighbouring samples and is no more than the cap above the sampled maximum. A larger jump means the sweep is too coarse to trust a fit.
  • With 100 points the refinement is typically well under 0.1 %. The gap between fitted and sampled Pmax, divided by √3, is carried into the uncertainty as u_disc.

6 · Measurement uncertainty

Why. Calling a module bad means its result is lower than the measurement itself could explain. Each result therefore carries an uncertainty, and every screening rule takes it into account.

How. The STC result is recomputed with each input nudged by its standard uncertainty: irradiance calibration, irradiance repeatability and temperature. The change in Pmax and FF is that input's contribution. This numerically estimates the effect of assumed input uncertainties, but it does not quantify correction-model, coefficient, or spectral mismatch errors. Contributions are combined in quadrature, following the GUM (independent inputs), and multiplied by the coverage factor k:

u(P) = √( u_G² + u_G,rep² + u_T² + u_meter² + u_disc² ) u(FF) = √( u_G² + u_G,rep² + u_T² + u_disc² + u_end² ) U = k · u (k = , provisional)
  • Multiplicative tracer gain errors approximately cancel in FF for uniform gain across a sweep; voltage/current offsets and nonlinearity may not cancel.
  • Irradiance calibration affects power almost one-for-one, and it is usually the largest term.
  • Common versus random. Calibration and tracer gain errors may be shared when the same setup is used for the entire batch, though drift and day-to-day variation can make them partially uncorrelated. The population test (section 7) uses only the random part. The absolute Ne3 checks (section 8) use everything.

7 · Population outliers (modified z-score)

Why compare with the population? Modules made from the same cell batch and process should be alike. Comparing them with each other also cancels every error they share, such as sensor calibration, tracer gain and spectrum. That makes this the most sensitive test for a single damaged module.

Why median and MAD, not mean and standard deviation? One very bad module inflates the standard deviation, which makes itself look less unusual. This is called masking. The median and the median absolute deviation (MAD) are barely moved by up to half the data being bad.

z = (x − median) / σ_eff σ_eff = max( 1.4826 · MAD , median of the group's u_random(x) ) u_random = √( u_G,rep² + u_T² + u_disc² [+ u_end² for FF] )
  • 1.4826 · MAD estimates the standard deviation for normally distributed data, so z reads like an ordinary z-score.
  • The floor stops a very tight population from turning measurement noise into outliers. This floor reduces false flags in a very tight sample, but the resulting score is a heuristic, not a formal hypothesis test.
  • z is computed for W/cell and FF separately. Low outliers (z ≤ −) are flagged for retest, and z ≤ − for review.
  • Groups smaller than modules give an unstable median and MAD, so they skip this test.

8 · Expected Ne3 performance

Why. If a whole batch was damaged, for example by a lamination problem, every module looks normal against the others. An absolute check may catch that only when reference specifications and CTM ratio are appropriate for your builds.

W/cell limit = P_cell · (CTM / 100) · (1 − shortfall / 100) Voc/cell limit = Voc_cell · (1 − tolerance / 100) Isc limit = Isc_cell · (1 − tolerance / 100)

With the current settings: W/cell ≥ , FF ≥ %, Voc/cell ≥ V, Isc per string ≥ A.

  • Cell-to-module (CTM) ratio covers encapsulation reflection and absorption, and ribbon losses. The best value comes from a few known-good modules: measured W/cell ÷ bare-cell Pmax.
  • A module is flagged Retest when even its result plus the displayed provisional U is below the limit; this is a screening heuristic, not a statistical guarantee. If only the result itself is below, it gets Review.
  • The defaults are typical Ne3 values (≈ 3.70 W, Voc ≈ 0.725 V, FF ≈ 81 % bare cell). Use your supplier's flash data or bin labels when you have them.

10 · When a module is flagged

  1. Re-sweep first. Check the sensor alignment, temperature reading, Ns entry and clip contacts, then sweep again. Many flags disappear here.
  2. If it repeats, inspect visually. Look for cracks, delamination, discoloured or burnt spots, lifted ribbons and damaged connectors.
  3. Electroluminescence (EL) shows cracks, inactive areas and broken fingers that are invisible to the eye.
  4. Infrared (IR) in sun or under forward bias shows hot spots from shunts or high-resistance joints.
  5. Check the electrical safety basics. Check bypass paths, connectors and insulation resistance before putting a module back on the car.
  6. Record the decision. Export the screening CSV and note what was found next to each flagged module.

Review means re-sweep when convenient and keep an eye on the module. A high outlier almost always means an entry or sensor error rather than an exceptional module.

11 · Limitations

  • Calibrated reference needed. Preset Ne3 thresholds and coefficients are provisional; do not interpret OK as reuse certification.
  • Spectrum is not corrected. Morning, evening and hazy sun differ in spectrum from AM1.5. Modules swept together share the error, but comparisons across days are weaker.
  • Procedure 1 is a model. Its accuracy falls as conditions move away from reference, and it relies on the device-specific Rs and κ; assumed coefficients, missing endpoint extrapolation, and model error are not fully captured in U.
  • Uniform light is assumed. Curved modules, or modules lit at a steep angle, receive less light than a flat sensor reports.
  • Repeat measurements. Repeated sweeps are collapsed to one module for population screening; empirical repeatability is computed when two or more sweeps are provided. The default uncertainty still does not include physical degradation or predictive reliability.
  • Shunts and steps are not auto-detected. Look at the curves.
  • This is screening, not certification. Electrical safety, insulation and mechanical integrity need their own checks.
